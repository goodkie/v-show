Trade Show Booth 3D Capture Guide

Follow these simple smartphone or DSLR photography instructions to ensure seamless, photo-realistic 3D Gaussian Splatting neural reconstruction.

📸 Recommended Photo Count: 60–100 Photographs

To capture every product angle and brand signage clearly, aim for 60 to 100 high-resolution photos taken from various heights around your booth perimeter.

  • Use 4K or 2K photo resolution on your smartphone or camera.
  • Maintain consistent lighting (avoid flash or rapid exposure shifts).
  • Ensure 60% visual overlap between successive adjacent photos.
Phase 1: Outer Orbit

Full Perimeter Orbit (Eye-Level)

Walk a complete 360-degree loop around your physical booth at normal eye-level (1.5–1.7m), capturing photos every 1–2 steps:

  1. Front Wide View
  2. Front-Left Corner
  3. Left Flank
  4. Rear-Left Corner
  5. Rear Wall & Infrastructure
  6. Rear-Right Corner
  7. Right Flank
  8. Front-Right Corner
Phase 2: High & Low Angles

Overhead & Low Angles

Perform a second loop holding the camera high (aimed slightly downward to capture booth ceiling/truss signage) and a third loop crouching slightly to capture lower product shelves and counter bases.

Phase 3: Product Highlights

Close-Up Product Hotspot Passes

For key machinery or flagship products on display, step inward and take 3–5 dedicated close-ups from multiple angles so our team can attach high-precision 3D clickable hotspots.

🚫 What to Avoid

  • Avoid people walking through frames: Capture photos during quiet pre-show hours or morning setup.
  • Do not move objects: Keep chairs, promotional items, and display units in the same position during capture.
  • Do not submit video grid screenshots: Always provide original, uncompressed camera photos (.jpg / .png).
